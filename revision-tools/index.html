A simple way to begin a revision session

Start with one topic or a small set of cards. Try to answer before you reveal the explanation, and notice which ideas need another look. A short quiz can help you find gaps; it is a prompt for practice, not a prediction of an exam result. If you get stuck, switch to a book or a video lesson, then return to the question in your own words.

Flashcards for active recall

Review a starter set or create your own question-and-answer cards. Flip each card, check your understanding and mark what you want to revisit.

Topic quizzes for a quick check

Try the available one-question knowledge checks for a selected starter topic. Each gives a brief explanation; take time to read it, whether your answer was right or wrong.

Practice tests from your cards

Choose a set and a short test length of up to 5, 10 or 20 questions. Review the answers afterwards. These tests use the selected flashcard set rather than an exam-board question bank.

Change approach when rereading is not helping

Use the book shelf for hosted text editions, or browse selected video lessons for another voice or example. A short practice set can then help you check whether the idea makes sense without looking at the source. The tools are intended to support a learner’s own course and teacher guidance, not to replace them.

  1. Pick a specific question. Name the small idea you want to remember rather than trying to revise a whole subject at once.
  2. Try to recall it first. Answer a card or question from memory, then compare your response with the provided answer.
  3. Check your course coverage. Starter topics and public community sets are not a complete exam specification. Use your teacher’s materials and the specification for your exam board.
Scope matters. The current starter material is introductory. A correct answer in a short practice tool does not show that every part of a course has been covered, and no score is a guarantee of an exam outcome.

Revision tool questions

Are these revision tools free?

Everything Hub’s revision activities are available in its free online workspace. Sign in to use the workspace and save supported account progress. External books, videos and linked study websites are controlled by their own providers.

Can I create my own flashcards?

Yes. Add your own question-and-answer pairs to a set. The card editor does not generate or check a complete exam course for you, so keep your answers aligned with your teacher’s materials.

Does Everything Hub include every exam subject?

No. Starter topics are a small introduction, not full coverage. Use the course specification and your school’s resources to decide what you need to revise.
