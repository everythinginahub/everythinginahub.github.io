# Everything Hub

A free-to-host personal workspace on GitHub Pages, with Supabase accounts and progress sync. Visitors see a sign-in screen; the workspace requires sign-in. The searchable home screen brings together everyday lists, habits, saved links, tasks, notes, books, timers and calculators alongside the existing school timetable, revision, games, rewards and shared community chat. Existing accounts, progress, revision routes and deep links remain in place. Selecting school subjects is no longer required for signup; people can select subjects when they first open revision tools.

## Everyday spaces

- **Lists:** create up to 10 named checklists with up to 40 items each; search lists and items, filter by completion, add multiple items at once, reorder, edit and check items. Duplicate, reset or copy a list, and undo the most recent removal or deletion while the page is open. A duplicate starts unchecked; clipboard access depends on the browser. List progress is saved with account progress; search, drafts and undo are only kept while the Lists page is open.
- **Habits:** create up to 12 habits, including archived habits, with daily, selected-weekday or weekly-count schedules. Add descriptions, colours and icons, reorder or archive habits, and filter those due today. Check in or undo on a monthly calendar for the past 90 calendar days; up to 180 check-in dates per habit are retained and the calendar can show the current and previous 11 months. Weekly progress and recorded streaks use the current schedule, not a lifetime history; changing the schedule can change these figures. Only a check-in for today can earn the usual daily habit reward. Optional reminders require explicit permission and device opt-in, and work only while the Habits page is open; they are not background or closed-tab notifications. Dates use the device’s local time.
- **Bookmarks:** save, edit and remove up to 40 HTTP(S) website addresses. Organise bookmarks in named folders, mark favourites, search and sort your collection. Folders are created by naming them on a bookmark and disappear from the filter when empty. Existing Saved links remain available. This is a hub collection, not access to your browser’s built-in bookmarks. Links open external sites in a new tab; those sites are not reviewed or controlled by the hub. Do not store sensitive links or information.
- **Tasks:** the existing homework planner is now a general task list. Previously saved homework and the old `#planner` link are preserved. Completing a task no longer records a study day; past activity is unchanged.
- **Everyday tools:** the existing focus timer, calculator, percentage checker, word counter, unit converter and date countdown remain. The new bill splitter rounds the per-person amount up to cover the bill and tip (there may be a small remainder). The temperature converter supports Celsius, Fahrenheit and Kelvin. A random picker chooses between up to 30 options for fun, and a text formatter changes case or tidies spaces. These tools are session-only; they do not send notifications or provide currency conversion.

Lists, habits, links, tasks, notes and timetable share the existing account progress JSON and its **100,000-byte overall limit**. Habit history uses part of that shared limit (12 full 180-date histories alone take roughly 28 KB of JSON), so large accounts may reach it sooner. Sync is last-write-wins across devices, and a sync error leaves a pending local copy to resolve in the account menu. They are not shared with other users, but are not end-to-end encrypted and may be accessible to the service operator; avoid sensitive information. Browser testing used a mock account backend, not a live cross-device Supabase session. New username accounts use recovery codes rather than confirmation emails, as described below.

## Import a timetable file

Open **Timetable → Import a timetable**, choose a PDF (up to 5 pages) or a clear, straight JPG, PNG or WebP (up to 12 MB, images under 40 megapixels), then scan it. Text PDFs are read with local PDF.js; pages without recognisable timetable text are rendered and read with local Tesseract OCR. The importer attempts to interpret English weekday grids, simple day-and-time lists, and PDF layouts with separately stacked day-and-period cards. When recognised, teacher names and rooms are kept separate from the subject; they remain editable and optional. It may miss or misread text, times, rooms and unusual layouts; check every suggested subject, day, time, teacher and room, correct or remove rows, or add missing ones before confirming. Password-protected PDFs are not supported. Missing times and days need to be filled in. Rotas, alternating weeks, durations and non-weekly dates are not automatically supported. No existing entries are replaced. Entries with the same subject, weekday and start time are treated as duplicates even if their rooms differ. The combined timetable still has a 60-lesson limit.

The file and recognised text remain in the browser and are discarded on leaving the page; neither is uploaded or saved. The browser first downloads the PDF reader or OCR code and English model from the site as needed. Only confirmed lesson details are stored with account progress and synced under the existing privacy and storage limits. OCR works best on clear screenshots or printed text; handwriting, low contrast, tilted images, decorative grids and non-English text may require manual entry. Browser tests used synthetic timetable images and a mocked account, not real cross-device sync. OCR and PDF asset sources and licenses are documented in `public/ocr/README.md` and `public/pdf/README.md`.

## Community chat nicknames

New accounts choose a unique 2–24 character username at signup. The username can be used as a community chat nickname when it passes the chat rules; choose something that does not identify you. To change it, click the profile icon, then **Settings**. Changes apply to new community messages; earlier messages retain the nickname used when they were sent. Nicknames are stored with the account and checked by the server, which ignores any nickname submitted with an individual message. Existing accounts are asked to choose a nickname in settings before posting: their old signup name is not automatically published because it may contain personal information. Automatic filtering is limited and can reject harmless names or miss harmful content. Live-game player nicknames are separate from community chat. Restricted names include Shay and common variations, and titles that could impersonate staff or the owner; only the confirmed owner account may use the exact name Shay (case-insensitive). The same server-side check applies to account and live-game nicknames. Other restricted account nicknames must be changed in profile settings before posting. Old community messages with restricted names are displayed as “Member”, except the confirmed owner’s exact Shay nickname. This is a limited filter and cannot catch every possible lookalike. The Owner badge in community and game-room chat is assigned server-side to the confirmed owner account, not based on the chosen nickname; the account email is not displayed to other chat users.

## AI chat

The AI chat is a native interface, not an embed. The refreshed workspace includes optional question ideas that fill the composer without sending, a character counter, and a clear-chat button. Its authenticated Supabase Edge Function calls Gemini 3.5 Flash-Lite with a server-only key. A confirmed account can make up to 10 attempts per UTC day, reserved atomically before a provider call; failed or cancelled calls can count. Clearing chat does not reset the allowance. Only the current question is sent. The browser holds the displayed conversation in memory, not account progress; the quota table stores only account ID, UTC date and count, not prompts. Questions go to Google and are subject to its terms and data practices; Google may use free-tier content to improve its products. Do not send personal or sensitive information; check important answers. The feature is not suitable for unsupervised children. `#kindle-ai` remains an alias.

The per-account cap is not a guaranteed equal share of Google's shared quota: many accounts can exhaust it before others arrive; multiple accounts can evade a per-account cap. Google controls project/model request and token quotas and their reset times. We do not set an extra numeric site-wide limit; the provider's available quota is the maximum. Quotas can change or be shared with other uses of the same project. Billing should stay disabled for a free-tier-only project; enabling billing can incur charges. Consider signup abuse, safeguarding and Google/Supabase limits before public use.

**Deployment required before this chat will work:**
1. Run [`supabase/ai_quota.sql`](supabase/ai_quota.sql) in the project's Supabase SQL Editor. It grants only the service role access to reserve requests; do not grant clients access.
2. In Supabase Dashboard → Edge Functions → Secrets, add `GEMINI_API_KEY` with the Gemini key. Never put this value in the repository, browser code, or chat. Supabase supplies `SUPABASE_URL` and `SUPABASE_SERVICE_ROLE_KEY` to functions automatically.
3. Deploy `supabase/functions/ai-chat` using Supabase CLI: `supabase functions deploy ai-chat --project-ref <your-project-ref>` (authenticate with the CLI separately). Keep JWT verification enabled. The function also verifies the token and email confirmation server-side before reserving quota.
4. Verify with a real confirmed account and check Google AI Studio's model-specific quota. The implementation has only mock tests; it has not been exercised against the live function or Gemini. Provider, project configuration, free-tier availability and service limits may prevent responses.

Notes save as you type and on leaving the page; trash is retained until permanently deleted and counts towards the limit. Existing notes are preserved. There are up to 30 notes (including archived/trashed), with titles up to 80 characters, text bodies up to 1,000 characters, or checklists of up to 20 items of 100 characters each. This is not Google Keep: reminders, attachments and collaboration are not supported. **Timetable** supports up to 60 editable weekly lessons with a day, local start time and optional room.
- **Everyday tools** includes a 1–90 minute focus timer, a basic arithmetic calculator with brackets, a score-to-percentage calculator, word counter, metric unit converter and calendar-day countdown. The focus timer is session-only, resets on leaving the page and does not send notifications. Percentages are not grade predictions.

Tasks, notes and timetable entries are stored in the same account progress JSON as revision, subject to its overall 100,000-byte limit and last-write-wins syncing. A sync error leaves a pending local copy; use the account menu to resolve it. Notes and timetables are not shared with other learners, but are not end-to-end encrypted and may be accessible to the service operator; avoid sensitive information. Neither the new tools nor chat have been verified in a real cross-device browser session.

The launcher is inspired by the general all-in-one feel of KindleHub, without copying its assets or targeting Kindle devices. Everything Hub does not provide KindleHub's encrypted group chat: its Chat remains the existing shared, moderated-by-limited-rules community conversation described below.

The library has 115 starter sets with 2,300 original cards across all 67 listed subjects (20 cards per set), plus a custom-subject option. These are **introductory examples, not complete or exam-board-specific coverage**; custom subjects may not have ready-made cards. Learners can create sets for their subjects and choose whether they are private or public to signed-in users. The library shows up to 100 of a learner's latest sets and 100 recent public sets. Public sets are unmoderated user-generated content: do not put personal information in them, and check accuracy independently. The Video lessons section includes 34 selected third-party YouTube lessons, playable in the site with an accompanying flashcard suggestion, plus subject-specific YouTube searches (whose results are not reviewed). Thumbnails load from YouTube in the video library; choosing a lesson loads a privacy-enhanced YouTube player. ## Study games

Signed-in learners can choose a starter, private or available public flashcard set and play **Match up**, **Quick quiz** or **Write & reveal**. Matching connects questions and answers; the quiz offers up to four choices and feedback; written recall is self-marked so different valid wording is possible. Rounds are shuffled and untimed, with in-round streaks and a best streak on completion. Quiz rounds with at least two missed cards and recall rounds with missed cards offer a focused retry. Duplicate questions or answers are excluded to avoid ambiguous choices; matching and the quiz need at least two distinct pairs, while written recall can use one. Game scores and typed responses are not saved; finishing a round records a study-activity day but does not change individual flashcard review marks. Leaving a game discards the round. Community sets are unmoderated and their accuracy should be checked.

Game design draws on the Education Endowment Foundation's discussion of [low-stakes retrieval and feedback](https://educationendowmentfoundation.org.uk/news/voices-from-the-classroom-beyond-the-quiz-feedback-and-retrieval-practice), without claiming guaranteed outcomes. The controls use ordinary keyboard-operable buttons and do not require speed, drag gestures or audio.

## Live multiplayer games

Signed-in learners can host a live game from **Study games → Play live with friends**, or from a flashcard set. Everyone needs an account: choose a nickname, share a 10-character invite code or link, and join the lobby. The host is also a player. A game needs at least two players and supports at most 30 (including the host), subject to Supabase capacity. Joining closes when the game starts; existing players can reconnect with the same account and code. The host can remove a player, reveal or advance a hosted quiz, and end a game. Removed accounts cannot rejoin that game.

There are four modes, each using up to 5, 10 or 20 distinct questions from the chosen set. **Treasure Tussle** awards a mystery chest for each correct answer: find 25–100 gold (60%), attempt to steal up to 50 gold from the richest other player (25%), or find a shield blocking one steal (15%). If there is no gold to steal, find 25 gold instead; shields do not stack. All three chest choices have identical odds. Most gold wins. **Rocket Rally** awards 10 points for the first correct answer in a streak, then 15, 20, 25 and up to 30 per answer; a wrong answer resets the streak. Most points wins. Both are self-paced. The classic **Host-led quiz** has everyone answer together, with the host revealing and advancing; **Flashcard race** is self-paced. The classic modes award one point per correct answer. For all self-paced modes, finishing time breaks ties. Scores are for the current game only; they do not change review marks or award coins. Questions and distractors come from the set and are not exam-board verified. This is casual multiplayer practice, not a cheat-proof examination system or a full Blooket clone; guest joining, other power-ups, and Blooket's proprietary modes and assets are not implemented.

Every game room has a **room chat** for its signed-in players, including in the lobby and after the game ends. Messages are plain text (up to 250 characters) and visible to everyone who joins. Players can delete their own messages; the host can delete any message, pause or resume chat, and remove players (which also removes their messages). Chat is rate-limited to one message every three seconds and 15 per minute per player. The server retains at most 200 messages per room and shows the latest 60. Messages expire with the room and are deleted when expired rooms are next cleaned up; they are not immediately deleted when the game ends. English-language rule checks block some harmful words, links and contact details in new room messages; older stored messages are also filtered when displayed. These checks can miss unsafe content and block harmless text. Use trusted invitations, avoid sharing personal information, and supervise where appropriate.

Updates use authenticated polling about every 2.5 seconds while the game screen is open, not instantaneous delivery; network interruptions may delay updates. Games expire after two hours and their stored questions, nicknames and answers are deleted opportunistically when another game is created after expiry. Each account may have at most three active hosted games and create at most ten games per two hours. Hosting explicitly shares questions and answers from the selected set with everyone who joins, **including a private set**. Codes are invitations, not a substitute for moderation; use non-identifying nicknames and only share codes with intended participants. Community content is not reviewed. Consider safeguarding, abuse prevention and Supabase free-tier limits before public or classroom use.

## Community chat and moderation

Signed-in users can open **Community chat** from the home screen without joining a game. It is one shared conversation visible to every signed-in user, not a private message or game room. Choose a non-identifying nickname (nicknames are not verified). Messages are plain text, limited to 250 characters, and displayed for up to 24 hours; the newest 100 are shown and storage is capped at 1,000. Expired messages and reports are deleted opportunistically on a subsequent send, not by a scheduled job. Users can delete their own messages. One report immediately hides a message from the reporter; three distinct reports hide it from everyone. Reports and hidden messages remain stored until cleanup or author deletion; there is no human-review dashboard or guarantee of review. Sending is limited to one message per three seconds and 15 per minute per account, and reporting to ten per hour.

A server-side rule filter checks community nicknames and messages, and live-room messages, before posting. It blocks some English profanity and harmful phrases, links, handles and long digit sequences. It can be bypassed, misses many forms of abuse and other languages, and can block harmless text; it is not AI moderation or a safety guarantee. Community chat is not appropriate for unsupervised children. Before public use, arrange human moderation, safeguarding and an incident-response process; consider the risk of coordinated false reports. Avoid personal or contact information and report unsafe messages.

## Practice tests, streak and rewards

Selecting a flashcard set opens its cards and study options: flashcards, games and a practice test. There is no separate practice-test library; older `#tests` links open the flashcard library, while set-specific `#tests/<id>` links still work. Each set links to an untimed multiple-choice practice test with a choice of up to 5, 10 or 20 questions. Questions and distractors come from the set itself, not an exam board or AI; other choices can sometimes be plausible. Learners can change answers before submission, leave questions blank (counted as incorrect), review all answers and retry. At least two unique pairs and one answered question are required. Tests and scores are session-only; completing a test records study activity but does not change flashcard review marks. Flashcards and the games hub also link to a set's quick quiz and games.

The Revision app shows a local-calendar streak and the last seven days of study. A virtual, non-monetary rewards shop awards coins for everyday activities (completing a task, checking a habit or list item, turning a page in a hosted text edition, and finishing an original game) as well as optional study activities. Each activity awards coins once per local day, up to 60 coins; undoing and repeating an action does not award extra coins. The original-game reward applies when a game is naturally won or lost, not when paused, restarted or exited. External embedded games and Internet Archive scans do not award coins. Coins unlock cosmetic account badges only; they are not money, prizes or secure currency. Existing progress receives no retroactive coins. The balance, badges and daily claims are part of the private progress JSON; like other progress, simultaneous cross-device edits are last-write-wins and may overwrite one another. Tests use mocks, not a live multi-device transaction test.

Learners can paste their own tab-separated question-and-answer pairs into a set draft (up to 50); it is not an AI importer. Gizmo and Quizlet have additional products such as AI generation and tutoring, advanced spaced repetition, additional multiplayer features and native mobile apps; those are not implemented here. For the researched products' current study modes, see [Quizlet's study guide](https://help.quizlet.com/hc/en-us/articles/360030841732-Studying-on-Quizlet) and [Gizmo's overview](https://gizmo.ai/).

## Supabase setup

1. Create a free Supabase project. Run [`supabase/setup.sql`](supabase/setup.sql), [`supabase/flashcard_sets.sql`](supabase/flashcard_sets.sql), [`supabase/live_games.sql`](supabase/live_games.sql), [`supabase/live_fun_modes.sql`](supabase/live_fun_modes.sql), [`supabase/live_chat.sql`](supabase/live_chat.sql), and then [`supabase/community_chat.sql`](supabase/community_chat.sql), followed by [`supabase/community_nicknames.sql`](supabase/community_nicknames.sql), and finally [`supabase/community_owner_names.sql`](supabase/community_owner_names.sql) in its SQL Editor. The flashcard script adds the table and row-level policies: authenticated users can read their own and public sets, but only their own sets can be changed or deleted. Anonymous users have no access. The progress table has separate per-user policies. Live-game tables deny direct browser access; authenticated, membership-checked RPCs enforce the game rules.
2. Apply `supabase/username_recovery.sql` followed by `supabase/username_auth.sql` after the owner functions, and deploy the username-auth Edge Function. Disable direct signup in Authentication while keeping email/password sign-in enabled. Keep email confirmation and secure email changes enabled for existing real-email accounts.
3. Set the Project URL and **publishable key** (or legacy `anon` key) in [`public/config.js`](public/config.js). Never publish a `service_role` or `sb_secret_` key, database password, SMTP credentials, or personal access token.
4. Test signup, username login, recovery, a used-code rejection, and progress sync with a disposable account. Existing account owners should sign in and generate recovery codes before relying on recovery. Existing guest data remains on its original device and is not automatically imported.

New username signups do not need SMTP. The legacy email sender is still restricted to team addresses and two emails per hour; do not rely on it for existing-account email recovery without configuring and testing custom SMTP. Existing owner access and the existing owner account UUID are retained.

The free tier has limits, may pause after inactivity, and does not include automatic backups. Review [Supabase pricing](https://supabase.com/pricing), privacy and safeguarding requirements, email delivery, backup strategy, account recovery, moderation and security before inviting real users, particularly children. Simultaneous edits on different devices are last-write-wins. Failed syncs remain on the device and the account menu lets the learner explicitly choose local or cloud progress.

## Private site status

`/status/` hosts the dashboard (`/public/status.html` is also available). The confirmed owner can sign in to the main site, open the dashboard and reveal its generated share code. Anyone with that code can view the aggregate counts without an account; do not share it publicly. The code is generated and kept in Supabase, not in the repository or URL; the status page does not load Clarity. No self-service code rotation is implemented.

The independent counter starts at deployment, with no earlier history. It counts main-site page loads and distinct in-app route changes (rapid repeats may be throttled), separately recording views while signed in. Authentication notifications and status-page visits do not add views; the status page still sends presence updates. Historical totals may contain earlier status views and duplicate counts, which cannot be reliably removed. The status page refreshes after its presence ping, when it becomes visible, every 30 seconds, and on demand; it shows a warning if its presence cannot be updated. A random browser identifier estimates distinct browsers and an account ID counts distinct accounts. An ephemeral tab ID and a heartbeat while the tab is visible estimate online guest browsers and signed-in accounts seen in the last three minutes. A person using multiple devices, or both guest and signed-in sessions, can be counted more than once. Browser storage clearing, blocked scripts, network failures, automated traffic and forged calls affect accuracy; this is not a tamper-proof analytics system. The database retains browser and account identifiers for these lifetime counts; stale session rows are deleted opportunistically after one day. No page paths, text, IP addresses or emails are stored by this tracker. The dashboard is separate from Microsoft Clarity, which has its own data collection and privacy settings. Review disclosure and consent requirements, especially for children, before using this analytics system.

To deploy, run `supabase/site_status.sql` after the owner SQL. The tables deny direct browser access and the restricted functions use the confirmed owner check or the code; keep the owner account secure. Anonymous tracking endpoints are public and can be abused, so monitor Supabase usage and consider server-side rate limiting before scaling.

## Username and recovery security

New accounts use a unique, case-insensitive username and password. Shay is reserved for the existing confirmed owner account. Login accepts the existing account's email for backwards compatibility. Passwords must have at least 8 characters and at most 72 UTF-8 bytes; longer unique passwords stored in a password manager are recommended. Usernames must begin with a letter and contain 2–24 ASCII letters, numbers or underscores. Owner/staff impersonation variants are restricted. Community chat nicknames are separate and are still subject to the existing server-side rules.

Each new user gets five one-use recovery codes. They must save them privately when shown; they cannot be retrieved later. Signed-in users can replace their codes through the account menu, which invalidates all previous codes. A valid code allows a password reset and is consumed before the reset is attempted, so a network failure may consume it without completing the reset. If both the password and all recovery codes are lost, access may be permanently lost. Existing users must sign in and generate their own codes; the owner account is not altered. These codes are as sensitive as passwords. Never share them with anyone.

Supabase Auth still performs password authentication and keeps existing sessions and account UUIDs. New username accounts use a random, non-deliverable internal address solely because Supabase Auth requires one. No user-provided email is auto-confirmed. Password sign-in is subject to Supabase's rate limits; a server-side global cap also limits username resolution, signup and recovery attempts, but hostile traffic can exhaust it for everyone. CAPTCHA, per-client signup limits, MFA and independent penetration testing are not implemented. There is no guarantee against account compromise. Do not rely on this service for sensitive information. Usernames can be enumerated, and the username resolver returns the associated internal address (or the existing email for legacy accounts), so do not treat usernames or email addresses as secret. The service role key stays server-side.

For setup apply `supabase/username_recovery.sql` first, then `supabase/username_auth.sql`, after the existing owner functions. Deploy the bundled `username-auth` Edge Function with JWT verification disabled for public signup and recovery; the function independently validates the user for code regeneration. Disable public email signup in Supabase Auth while retaining email/password login, confirmation, and secure email changes. End-to-end testing requires a disposable account; do not test recovery against the real owner account without first safely saving that account's recovery codes.
