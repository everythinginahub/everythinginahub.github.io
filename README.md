# Everything Hub

A free-to-host personal workspace on GitHub Pages, with Supabase accounts and progress sync. Visitors see a sign-in screen; the workspace requires sign-in. The searchable home screen brings together everyday lists, habits, saved links, tasks, notes, books, timers and calculators alongside the existing school timetable, revision, games, rewards and shared community chat. Existing accounts, progress, revision routes and deep links remain in place. Selecting school subjects is no longer required for signup; people can select subjects when they first open revision tools.

## Everyday spaces

- **Lists:** create up to 10 named checklists with up to 40 items each; search lists and items, filter by completion, add multiple items at once, reorder, edit and check items. Duplicate, reset or copy a list, and undo the most recent removal or deletion while the page is open. A duplicate starts unchecked; clipboard access depends on the browser. List progress is saved with account progress; search, drafts and undo are only kept while the Lists page is open.
- **Habits:** create up to 12 habits and mark or undo a check-in for the current local day. The previous seven calendar days are shown; up to 90 check-in dates are retained per habit. There are no reminders or notifications.
- **Saved links:** save, edit and remove up to 40 HTTP(S) website addresses. Links open external sites in a new tab; those sites are not reviewed or controlled by the hub. Do not store sensitive links or information.
- **Tasks:** the existing homework planner is now a general task list. Previously saved homework and the old `#planner` link are preserved. Completing a task no longer records a study day; past activity is unchanged.
- **Everyday tools:** the existing focus timer, calculator, percentage checker, word counter, unit converter and date countdown remain. The new bill splitter rounds the per-person amount up to cover the bill and tip (there may be a small remainder). The temperature converter supports Celsius, Fahrenheit and Kelvin. A random picker chooses between up to 30 options for fun, and a text formatter changes case or tidies spaces. These tools are session-only; they do not send notifications or provide currency conversion.

Lists, habits, links, tasks, notes and timetable share the existing account progress JSON and its **100,000-byte overall limit**. Sync is last-write-wins across devices, and a sync error leaves a pending local copy to resolve in the account menu. They are not shared with other users, but are not end-to-end encrypted and may be accessible to the service operator; avoid sensitive information. Browser testing used a mock account backend, not a live cross-device Supabase session. Public signup still needs a suitable SMTP sender as described below.

## Home screen and Games app

The signed-in home screen has a local clock, task, habit and timetable widgets, a searchable grid of all apps and a quick-access dock. Apps open in focused views without a sidebar; use Home in the header to return to the launcher. The old Explore and other deep links remain available. The design takes inspiration from the general tablet-launcher idea, using original styling rather than KindleHub assets.

**Geometry Dash** links to an embedded version of the official Geometry Dash website, which offers a free browser demo of Stereo Madness, not the full game. The site is run by RobTop Games. The iframe loads its website when the page opens; availability, embedding permission, privacy practices and device support are controlled by the site. If it refuses to connect or does not load, use the new-tab link. External network access from the test environment is restricted, so live embedded gameplay has not been verified. The previous separate level maker has been removed from the interface; its saved account data and device-local drafts are not deleted. The old `#arcade/designer` link still opens the Geometry Dash page.

**Games** brings regular and study games together. Its home lets you choose the regular collection or study games, and also features three original adventures. **Skyline Sprint** now has a double jump and restores a heart for every eight coins. **Orbit Defender** gains twin shots from wave two and awards bonuses for hit chains. **Reef Breaker** rewards crystal chains and drops temporary paddle-widening power-ups. Keyboard and on-screen controls are supported, with dragging in Orbit and Reef. Pause or restart any time; originals pause when the page loses focus or is hidden. Current games are discarded on leaving. Best scores are stored per signed-in account on this device, not synced; clearing browser data removes them. Finishing an original game earns virtual reward coins once per day.

Games now highlights three locally hosted, creator-made games: JavaScript Racer, Radius Raid and Can’t Get Back. Their cards and the three original adventure cards use real gameplay frames; controls and device requirements are shown on each page. Their embedded players are isolated from hub account data, and embedded-game scores are session-only. Sources, licences and local modifications are documented in `public/games/README.md`. Study/live modes use simple icons, and the older third-party catalogue is shown as text cards rather than misleading screenshots.

The regular collection lists 96 games hosted by [sausi-7/games](https://github.com/sausi-7/games) (catalogue metadata copyright © 2026 Saurabh Singh, MIT; see `public/licenses/sausi-games-MIT.txt`). The games themselves are not bundled or controlled by Everything Hub. A game is requested from its creator only when selected, in a sandboxed cross-origin iframe; a new-tab link is available if embedding fails. They can load additional resources from other providers and are subject to third-party availability, content and privacy practices. The upstream registry was checked against the source tree but individual games and device compatibility have not all been verified. Some games may not work, and an internet connection is required. These games have no connection to account progress or study rewards. Existing `#arcade/<game>`, `#games/<set>` and live-game links remain supported.

## Import a timetable image

Open **Timetable → Add from an image**, choose a clear, straight JPG, PNG or WebP (up to 12 MB and 40 megapixels), then scan it. The importer uses local, self-hosted Tesseract OCR and attempts to interpret English weekday grids or simple day-and-time lists. It may miss or misread text, times, rooms and unusual layouts; check the image and every suggested row, correct or remove rows, or add missing ones before confirming. Missing times and days need to be filled in. Rotas, alternating weeks, durations and non-weekly dates are not automatically supported. No existing entries are replaced. Entries with the same subject, weekday and start time are treated as duplicates even if their rooms differ. The combined timetable still has a 60-lesson limit.

The image and recognized text remain in the browser and are discarded on leaving the page; neither is uploaded or saved. The browser must first download the OCR code and English model from the site. Only confirmed lesson details are stored with account progress and synced under the existing privacy and storage limits. OCR works best on clear screenshots or printed text; handwriting, low contrast, tilted images, decorative grids and non-English text may require manual entry. Browser tests used synthetic timetable images and a mocked account, not real cross-device sync. OCR asset sources and licenses are documented in `public/ocr/README.md`.

## eBooks

The ten starter books now open **locally hosted Project Gutenberg text editions** by default rather than relying on Internet Archive's iframe. Reading controls include page navigation, search, a progress indicator, font, size and theme settings. Position and settings are stored on the current device, per signed-in account, not synced. The complete texts and their Project Gutenberg licence notices are retained; source and attribution are listed in `public/books/README.md`. Historical editions can contain outdated language or ideas. An Internet connection is still needed to load a text from the site unless the browser has cached it; this is not an offline app.

The Internet Archive scan remains available as an optional, separate edition, as well as a link to the original item. Pasting an Archive item ID or URL still opens its embedded viewer, with a direct link as fallback; custom items do not have locally hosted copies. Internet Archive controls its reader, content, availability, borrowing requirements and privacy practices. Custom content and external search are not reviewed by Everything Hub and may be unsuitable for children. Some scans may require an Archive account or fail to embed.

## Getting around and finding things

The **Apps** button is available throughout the workspace; use it to search for an app, pin favourites, or return to a recent app. On a keyboard, Alt+A opens the switcher. Pins and recent apps are stored separately for each signed-in account on this device, not synced to other devices. Opening another app still leaves the current page, so session-only tools such as the focus timer reset as usual.

Tasks, saved links, revision resources and everyday tools can be searched in place. The book shelf supports title and author sorting, and the game collection has a control to clear both its search and category. The AI chat embed can be disconnected without leaving its page. These controls don't change saved account progress.

## AI chat

The AI chat app embeds the independently operated [Free AI Chat Space by Zacheus10](https://huggingface.co/spaces/Zacheus10/free-ai-chat) on Hugging Face after the user chooses to load it. The Space describes its model as Qwen3-4B-Instruct, not Gemini. It sends messages to that third-party service, has free GPU limits and queues, and can change, become unavailable or require sign-in. It is not moderated by Everything Hub and is not suitable for unsupervised children. Do not send sensitive information; verify important answers. The old `#kindle-ai` link still opens this page. Gemini's own consumer chat does not provide a supported embed for this site.

## Existing school and learning tools

- **Tasks** uses the existing study planner and preserves its tasks. Add or edit a title and optional date, star important tasks, filter the list, mark tasks complete or delete them. Stars are saved with the same account progress as the rest of the task. The list-first design is inspired by Google Tasks; it does not connect to or sync with Google Tasks.
- **Notes** has a Keep-inspired board with quick capture, colours, pins, text notes, checklists, comma-separated labels, search, archive and trash. Notes save as you type and on leaving the page; trash is retained until permanently deleted and counts towards the limit. Existing notes are preserved. There are up to 30 notes (including archived/trashed), with titles up to 80 characters, text bodies up to 1,000 characters, or checklists of up to 20 items of 100 characters each. This is not Google Keep: reminders, attachments and collaboration are not supported. **Timetable** supports up to 60 editable weekly lessons with a day, local start time and optional room.
- **Everyday tools** includes a 1–90 minute focus timer, a basic arithmetic calculator with brackets, a score-to-percentage calculator, word counter, metric unit converter and calendar-day countdown. The focus timer is session-only, resets on leaving the page and does not send notifications. Percentages are not grade predictions.

Tasks, notes and timetable entries are stored in the same account progress JSON as revision, subject to its overall 100,000-byte limit and last-write-wins syncing. A sync error leaves a pending local copy; use the account menu to resolve it. Notes and timetables are not shared with other learners, but are not end-to-end encrypted and may be accessible to the service operator; avoid sensitive information. Neither the new tools nor chat have been verified in a real cross-device browser session.

The launcher is inspired by the general all-in-one feel of KindleHub, without copying its assets or targeting Kindle devices. Everything Hub does not provide KindleHub's encrypted group chat: its Chat remains the existing shared, moderated-by-limited-rules community conversation described below.

The library has 115 starter sets with 2,300 original cards across all 67 listed subjects (20 cards per set), plus a custom-subject option. These are **introductory examples, not complete or exam-board-specific coverage**; custom subjects may not have ready-made cards. Learners can create sets for their subjects and choose whether they are private or public to signed-in users. The library shows up to 100 of a learner's latest sets and 100 recent public sets. Public sets are unmoderated user-generated content: do not put personal information in them, and check accuracy independently. The Video lessons section includes 34 selected third-party YouTube lessons, playable in the site with an accompanying flashcard suggestion, plus subject-specific YouTube searches (whose results are not reviewed). Thumbnails load from YouTube in the video library; choosing a lesson loads a privacy-enhanced YouTube player. YouTube and creators control playback, availability, adverts and data handling; an external YouTube link is provided if an embed is unavailable. Videos may not match every exam board.

The interface uses one-time scroll reveals for content entering the viewport, short page entrances and hover feedback. Content remains visible without JavaScript animation support, and motion is disabled for people who request reduced motion in their device settings.

## Study games

Signed-in learners can choose a starter, private or available public flashcard set and play **Match up**, **Quick quiz** or **Write & reveal**. Matching connects questions and answers; the quiz offers up to four choices and feedback; written recall is self-marked so different valid wording is possible. Rounds are shuffled and untimed, with in-round streaks and a best streak on completion. Quiz rounds with at least two missed cards and recall rounds with missed cards offer a focused retry. Duplicate questions or answers are excluded to avoid ambiguous choices; matching and the quiz need at least two distinct pairs, while written recall can use one. Game scores and typed responses are not saved; finishing a round records a study-activity day but does not change individual flashcard review marks. Leaving a game discards the round. Community sets are unmoderated and their accuracy should be checked.

Game design draws on the Education Endowment Foundation's discussion of [low-stakes retrieval and feedback](https://educationendowmentfoundation.org.uk/news/voices-from-the-classroom-beyond-the-quiz-feedback-and-retrieval-practice), without claiming guaranteed outcomes. The controls use ordinary keyboard-operable buttons and do not require speed, drag gestures or audio.

## Live multiplayer games

Signed-in learners can host a live game from **Study games → Play live with friends**, or from a flashcard set. Everyone needs an account: choose a nickname, share a 10-character invite code or link, and join the lobby. The host is also a player. A game needs at least two players and supports at most 30 (including the host), subject to Supabase capacity. Joining closes when the game starts; existing players can reconnect with the same account and code. The host can remove a player, reveal or advance a hosted quiz, and end a game. Removed accounts cannot rejoin that game.

There are four modes, each using up to 5, 10 or 20 distinct questions from the chosen set. **Treasure Tussle** awards a mystery chest for each correct answer: find 25–100 gold (60%), attempt to steal up to 50 gold from the richest other player (25%), or find a shield blocking one steal (15%). If there is no gold to steal, find 25 gold instead; shields do not stack. All three chest choices have identical odds. Most gold wins. **Rocket Rally** awards 10 points for the first correct answer in a streak, then 15, 20, 25 and up to 30 per answer; a wrong answer resets the streak. Most points wins. Both are self-paced. The classic **Host-led quiz** has everyone answer together, with the host revealing and advancing; **Flashcard race** is self-paced. The classic modes award one point per correct answer. For all self-paced modes, finishing time breaks ties. Scores are for the current game only; they do not change review marks or award coins. Questions and distractors come from the set and are not exam-board verified. This is casual multiplayer practice, not a cheat-proof examination system or a full Blooket clone; guest joining, other power-ups, and Blooket's proprietary modes and assets are not implemented.

Every game room has a **room chat** for its signed-in players, including in the lobby and after the game ends. Messages are plain text (up to 250 characters) and visible to everyone who joins. Players can delete their own messages; the host can delete any message, pause or resume chat, and remove players (which also removes their messages). Chat is rate-limited to one message every three seconds and 15 per minute per player. The server retains at most 200 messages per room and shows the latest 60. Messages expire with the room and are deleted when expired rooms are next cleaned up; they are not immediately deleted when the game ends. English-language rule checks block some harmful words, links and contact details in new room messages; older stored messages are also filtered when displayed. These checks can miss unsafe content and block harmless text. Use trusted invitations, avoid sharing personal information, and supervise where appropriate.

Updates use authenticated polling about every 2.5 seconds while the game screen is open, not instantaneous delivery; network interruptions may delay updates. Games expire after two hours and their stored questions, nicknames and answers are deleted opportunistically when another game is created after expiry. Each account may have at most three active hosted games and create at most ten games per two hours. Hosting explicitly shares questions and answers from the selected set with everyone who joins, **including a private set**. Codes are invitations, not a substitute for moderation; use non-identifying nicknames and only share codes with intended participants. Community content is not reviewed. Consider safeguarding, abuse prevention and Supabase free-tier limits before public or classroom use.

## Community chat and moderation

Signed-in users can open **Community chat** from the home screen without joining a game. It is one shared conversation visible to every signed-in user, not a private message or game room. Choose a non-identifying nickname (nicknames are not verified). Messages are plain text, limited to 250 characters, and displayed for up to 24 hours; the newest 100 are shown and storage is capped at 1,000. Expired messages and reports are deleted opportunistically on a subsequent send, not by a scheduled job. Users can delete their own messages. One report immediately hides a message from the reporter; three distinct reports hide it from everyone. Reports and hidden messages remain stored until cleanup or author deletion; there is no human-review dashboard or guarantee of review. Sending is limited to one message per three seconds and 15 per minute per account, and reporting to ten per hour.

A server-side rule filter checks community nicknames and messages, and live-room messages, before posting. It blocks some English profanity and harmful phrases, links, handles and long digit sequences. It can be bypassed, misses many forms of abuse and other languages, and can block harmless text; it is not AI moderation or a safety guarantee. Community chat is not appropriate for unsupervised children. Before public use, arrange human moderation, safeguarding and an incident-response process; consider the risk of coordinated false reports. Avoid personal or contact information and report unsafe messages.

## Practice tests, streak and rewards

Selecting a flashcard set opens its cards and study options: flashcards, games and a practice test. There is no separate practice-test library; older `#tests` links open the flashcard library, while set-specific `#tests/<id>` links still work. Each set links to an untimed multiple-choice practice test with a choice of up to 5, 10 or 20 questions. Questions and distractors come from the set itself, not an exam board or AI; other choices can sometimes be plausible. Learners can change answers before submission, leave questions blank (counted as incorrect), review all answers and retry. At least two unique pairs and one answered question are required. Tests and scores are session-only; completing a test records study activity but does not change flashcard review marks. Flashcards and the games hub also link to a set's quick quiz and games.

The Revision app shows a local-calendar streak and the last seven days of study. A virtual, non-monetary rewards shop awards coins for everyday activities (completing a task, checking a habit or list item, turning a page in a hosted text edition, and finishing an original game) as well as optional study activities. Each activity awards coins once per local day, up to 60 coins; undoing and repeating an action does not award extra coins. The original-game reward applies when a game is naturally won or lost, not when paused, restarted or exited. External embedded games and Internet Archive scans do not award coins. Coins unlock cosmetic account badges only; they are not money, prizes or secure currency. Existing progress receives no retroactive coins. The balance, badges and daily claims are part of the private progress JSON; like other progress, simultaneous cross-device edits are last-write-wins and may overwrite one another. Tests use mocks, not a live multi-device transaction test.

Learners can paste their own tab-separated question-and-answer pairs into a set draft (up to 50); it is not an AI importer. Gizmo and Quizlet have additional products such as AI generation and tutoring, advanced spaced repetition, additional multiplayer features and native mobile apps; those are not implemented here. For the researched products' current study modes, see [Quizlet's study guide](https://help.quizlet.com/hc/en-us/articles/360030841732-Studying-on-Quizlet) and [Gizmo's overview](https://gizmo.ai/).

## Supabase setup

1. Create a free Supabase project. Run [`supabase/setup.sql`](supabase/setup.sql), [`supabase/flashcard_sets.sql`](supabase/flashcard_sets.sql), [`supabase/live_games.sql`](supabase/live_games.sql), [`supabase/live_fun_modes.sql`](supabase/live_fun_modes.sql), [`supabase/live_chat.sql`](supabase/live_chat.sql), and then [`supabase/community_chat.sql`](supabase/community_chat.sql) in its SQL Editor. The flashcard script adds the table and row-level policies: authenticated users can read their own and public sets, but only their own sets can be changed or deleted. Anonymous users have no access. The progress table has separate per-user policies. Live-game tables deny direct browser access; authenticated, membership-checked RPCs enforce the game rules.
2. In **Authentication → URL Configuration**, set the Site URL to `https://reviselikeapro.github.io/public/` and add `https://reviselikeapro.github.io/public/login.html` to the Redirect URLs allowlist. Keep email confirmation enabled. When a confirmation link is opened, the browser returns to the login page rather than opening the workspace directly.
3. Set the Project URL and **publishable key** (or legacy `anon` key) in [`public/config.js`](public/config.js). These keys are intended for browser use only with row-level security enabled. Never publish a `service_role` or `sb_secret_` key, database password, SMTP credentials, or personal access token.
4. Configure a custom SMTP sender for public signups, then test signup, confirmation, sign-in, progress sync across two devices and private/public visibility with two accounts. The built-in Supabase email sender is intended for testing: it sends only to organisation team addresses and is limited to two emails per hour. A third-party SMTP service may offer a free tier but has separate limits, requirements and possible charges. See [Supabase's email testing guide](https://supabase.com/docs/guides/auth/testing-auth-flows). Email confirmation must remain enabled; do not bypass it to avoid configuring email.

For the existing project, the schemas and production confirmation redirect have already been configured, but **custom SMTP is still required for general public registration**. Existing guest data stays on its original device but is not automatically imported into an account and is no longer accessible in guest mode.

The free tier has limits, may pause after inactivity, and does not include automatic backups. Review [Supabase pricing](https://supabase.com/pricing), privacy and safeguarding requirements, email delivery, backup strategy, account recovery, moderation and security before inviting real users, particularly children. Simultaneous edits on different devices are last-write-wins. Failed syncs remain on the device and the account menu lets the learner explicitly choose local or cloud progress.

## Development

Requires Node.js 22.5 or newer. Run `npm ci`, then `npm start` and open http://localhost:3000. The bundled Node server serves the static site; the frontend account flow uses Supabase. For a local confirmation redirect, add the local `login.html` URL to the Supabase allowlist as well. The experimental Node/SQLite API remains available for development but is not used by the hosted Supabase account flow or GitHub Pages.

`npm test` runs unit, browser-DOM and local API tests. Account and multiplayer browser tests use mocks and do not prove live email delivery or a real cross-device session. Live-game RPCs and permissions have also been checked in rolled-back database transactions. `npm run build:client` rebuilds the checked-in browser Supabase bundle from pinned package dependencies. Its third-party licenses are in `public/licenses/`.

## Video and learning design notes

The selected player loads only after a learner chooses a lesson; the gallery uses lazy-loaded YouTube thumbnails. Embedded playback uses YouTube's privacy-enhanced domain and a referrer policy that sends the site origin, as required by YouTube. Embedding may be blocked by a video's owner, privacy tools, or regional restrictions; no automatic playback or guarantee of availability is assumed. See [YouTube's embedding guidance](https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/171780) and [player requirements](https://developers.google.com/youtube/player_parameters). The short recall prompt and related flashcards are informed by the Education Endowment Foundation's guidance on [retrieval and revision routines](https://educationendowmentfoundation.org.uk/news/eef-guest-blog-building-study-habits-and-revision-routines); they are not a claim of guaranteed outcomes.
